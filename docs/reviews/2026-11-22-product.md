# Product review: what would make Agent Blog awesome

2026-11-22, after M1. A read-only review by a second model (Fable), asked by the owner. **Ideas and arguments, not
decisions**: anything taken from here becomes a decision record or a roadmap line first.

## The shape of it

A correct, well-guarded publishing pipeline with an MCP door, ahead on safety (pinned approvals, stale-revision
refusals, schedules judged at fire time, mutation-tested guards) and behind on the relationship: the agent writes
blind (nothing tells it who the blog is for, how it sounds, what was rejected), and the owner can only say yes or no
(no reason on decline, no way to change a word in the app).

The real first user is a technical solo builder who lives in Claude or Claude Code and whose blog, changelog or devlog
is dead because publishing is a chore. "Niche site run end to end" is the spam persona; don't court it.

## The one lever: make the channel two-way

1. **Brief**: Account → This blog gains "How this blog sounds" (audience, voice, never-do list, exemplar posts),
   returned by `get_blog`. Owner-written, marked as the owner's, the one text an agent may treat as instruction.
2. **Decline with a reason, and "Ask for changes"**: a one-line note on the Inbox card, returned by `get_request`.
3. **Notes that persist**: every reason lands in a feedback log that `get_blog` surfaces, so the next session starts
   where the last was corrected.

An MCP door isn't a moat (WordPress is adding one); a blog that makes any agent write better on it over time is, and
it turns the levels into a trust story. Size M; the server still calls no model.

## Next, ranked by impact for effort

1. Owner quick-edit in the app, and "Approve with edits" (S–M).
2. Push notifications for the Inbox (S): without them an agent at Propose stalls silently.
3. A deterministic `check_post` pre-flight: links, alt text, excerpt, title length, headings, similar posts (S–M), shown on the Inbox card too.
4. Social cards and structured data: `og:image` per theme or first picture, JSON-LD (S).
5. Honest bylines: "Written by Claude, approved by …" from the revisions, per-blog setting (S).
6. Minimal email subscribe through the owner's own SMTP or Resend key, double opt-in (M).
7. A per-post timeline (revisions, notes, approvals), a word-level diff for prose, `list_feedback` (M).
8. An idea backlog: `propose_idea`, the owner taps "Write this" or "No" (S–M).
9. Series and internal links (S).
10. Earned autonomy as a prompt, never automatic: "You approved Claude's last 12 unchanged. Let it publish?" (S).
11. Hosted: "your blog in 60 seconds, from inside your chat"; custom domains early; invite-only until report and takedown exist (L).
12. Import from WordPress and Ghost, whose posts become the brief's exemplars (M).

## Cut or don't build (the review's view)

The reader theme bar beyond the demo blog; more or uploaded themes; agent `edit_blog_settings` and taxonomy upkeep
tools; several human authors; the Support sibling until the blog has outside users; comments.

## Risks

WordPress shipping MCP (answer: the collaboration loop, not the door); the hosted service as an AI-spam farm (invite
only, Publish and Full on hosted need a custom domain or a paid plan, report link, publish rate that queues);
prompt injection through fetched content (per-connection publish rate, a "pause this agent" button); the AI-slop
stigma (lead with the hand on the publish button and the byline); approval fatigue (check summaries, batching, the
earned-autonomy prompt); chat clients that can't PUT (treat `upload_media_from_url` as the main path, add owner
upload); solo-developer spread.

## Where it already wins, and where not yet

Wins: levels with pinned approvals and real undo against WordPress roles; any agent story at all against Ghost; owning
the data against Substack; the last mile against "just ask ChatGPT"; a safety model tested by breaking it.
Not yet: editing, distribution (no social image, no subscribe), import, any stats, and until the brief and feedback
exist, an agent writes no better here than in a chat window.

Suggested order: the feedback loop with quick-edit and push; then `check_post` and social cards; then bylines and
subscribe; then hosted.
